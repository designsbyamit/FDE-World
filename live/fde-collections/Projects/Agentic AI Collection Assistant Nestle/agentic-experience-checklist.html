Prototype audit
1

Intent, scope, and boundaries

Make the agent’s purpose, authority, and limits explicit before it acts.

Use plain language; do not require users to infer intent from an action label.
Set expectations before execution, not after a change occurs.
Never imply capability, access, or certainty the agent does not have.
Verify both the happy path and an attempted boundary violation.
2

Human control and consent

Keep the person in control at the level of risk, reversibility, and consequence.

The prompt names the action, affected records, impact, and confirm/cancel choices.
Match interaction weight to the action’s risk.
The system should continue with the user’s decision and preserve the rationale.
State remains understandable after interruption.
Show the delegation relationship and its current state.
3

Trust and transparency

Give people enough evidence to judge the recommendation and the work performed.

Explain drivers, not hidden chain-of-thought or opaque scores alone.
Confidence should change the interaction, not only decorate it.
Users can assess freshness and provenance at the point of use.
Use comprehensible activity history, with safe detail for the user’s role.
4

Execution, feedback, and recovery

Keep progress legible and make failure recoverable without hiding useful work.

A user should always know whether they need to act or simply wait.
Do not strand the person behind an indefinite spinner.
A failure in one step must not erase usable outcomes.
Contain errors to the failed dependency whenever possible.
5

Quality gate

Validate the experience as a complete, accessible, policy-aligned user journey.

Record the test plan or prototype walkthrough as evidence.
Include screen-reader names and focus order in the check.
Review high-stakes and error messages line by line.
No unresolved guardrail should be invisible at release time.
Use this page’s evidence fields or link the signed-off record.
Release decision: Do not ship with an unchecked consequential-control, transparency, or recovery requirement unless the accountable owner has documented and accepted the exception.
