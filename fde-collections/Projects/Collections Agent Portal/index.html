01 · Domain foundation

Understand Collections

An operating view of enterprise receivables: financial exposure, customer context, resolution workflows, governance boundaries and measurable cash outcomes.

Operating context

Once an invoice reaches contractual maturity, Collections determines why cash has not been received and coordinates the appropriate resolution. That may involve targeted customer engagement, dispute or deduction management, credit clarification, promise-to-pay monitoring, escalation, and reconciliation with Accounts Receivable.

Collections protects cash flow without compromising customer relationships or financial control.

Taxonomy

How every collections concept is classified.

Taxonomy is the shared filing system for collections. It gives product screens, filters, analytics and AI reasoning the same names for customers, financial objects, work, problems, risk, communication and outcomes.

How to read it

Start with one of seven domain categories. Within it, move from a concept—such as Payment—to an allowed type—such as Partial Payment.

Ontology vs taxonomy

Ontology says a payment clears an open item. Taxonomy classifies that payment as customer, advance, partial, overpayment or unallocated.

Company code model

One customer relationship, several legal entities, one collector-specific view.

A company code identifies the NorthStar legal entity that owns a financial document. A collection segment determines which company codes are in scope; partner assignment determines which customers a collector handles.

Collector→Assigned segment→Permitted company codes→AR documents→Assigned partners only
Alan's view

Assigned exposure: 300

C123 has 100 in company code 1000 and 200 in 2000. Alan sees one collapsed customer row and can expand it into those two company-code rows.

Mary's view

Assigned exposure: 700

C123 may rank above a 750 account because broader segment-level exposure raises its risk. Mary can act only on her assigned company code.

Open decision

Version 1.1 alternates between showing overall exposure in the summary and showing only collector-assigned totals. The meeting direction favours collector-scoped amounts with a note that other exposure exists. Product ownership must confirm the final rule.

Deeper data and currency detail

Authorisation: the partner must exist in UDMBPSEGMENTS for the collector's segment, and UDM_SGMT_COMP must map that segment to the company code.

Currency: company-code rows use T001-WAERS. Consolidated values use an approximate group-currency equivalent with SAP exchange-rate type M and a visible ~ marker.

Customer 360: assigned company codes can be consolidated or selected individually. Activity outside the collector's portfolio is contextual only—no line items, contacts, or actions are exposed.

Known data work: blocked-order mapping requires TVKO; company-code credit-balance support remains unresolved; credit limit and utilisation belong to credit-control-area data.

Current implementation

What the Unified Collections Agent actually does

It processes one customer per run. Configuration decides which intelligence steps run and where the collector must intervene.

Validate & configure→Predict arrears→Assess risk→Explain account→Recommend→Human trigger→Draft from fresh data→Human approval→Send & update

NorthStar configuration

  • Risk assessment enabled
  • Multiple eligible scenarios may be merged
  • Pre-generation and post-draft human checkpoints
  • X1/X2 items excluded from chasing content
  • Current implementation skips FSCM contact recording after send

Standard configuration

  • Email-context analysis enabled
  • One eligible scenario selected
  • No pre-generation pause
  • Post-draft approval when automatic sending is off
  • Collector contact recorded in FSCM after send
Implementation detail and known product tensions

The engineering record describes a shared LangGraph workflow whose nodes are enabled by tenant configuration. Email tables are built from fresh database queries rather than generated by the language model.

The implementation uses a nine-component deterministic NorthStar risk score and includes a Critical priority, while the product requirement describes four weighted factors and High / Medium / Low tiers. This remains an open reconciliation item.

Agent Runs and Account History are intentionally separate: the former captures system execution; the latter captures communication and human-visible account activity.

Learning & Optimisation

Learn from outcomes without experimenting recklessly.

Exploratory Reinforcement learning is not documented as a current product capability. The current agent produces useful feedback and outcome signals; a future learning policy would need separate validation and governance.

Simple explanation

A bounded system learns which permitted action tends to work best in a given situation.

Collections example

For a low-risk overdue case, choose among an approved email, a call recommendation, waiting, or human escalation—then observe payment, response, resolution, or harm.

Customer context→Allowed actions only→Recommendation→Human decision→Outcome→Offline learning
Current foundations

Signals already available

  • Approve, edit, regenerate, discard and skip
  • Recommendation cooldowns
  • Draft and send state
  • Agent-run status and outcomes
  • Payments, replies, promises and disputes
Recommended first model

Constrained contextual bandit

Choose one allowed action using the current case context. Evaluate it offline before any controlled, human-approved trial.

Not authorised

Unrestricted autonomous RL

Do not let a learner create actions, recipients, financial changes, coercive tactics or out-of-scope access.

A staged path to evidence

0Instrument decisions and outcomes
1Describe patterns; change nothing
2Evaluate policies offline
3Run in shadow mode
4Human-approved experiment
5Constrained production optimisation

Optimise for

Payment, useful response, kept promise, dispute progress, resolution, lower effort and appropriate collector acceptance.

Penalise separately

Complaints, duplicate contact, broken promises, policy violations, incorrect context, large rewrites and unnecessary escalation.

Critical caveat

Payment after an action does not prove the action caused it. The system must log eligible actions and selection probabilities, separate human edits, define outcome windows, and use off-policy evaluation before deployment.

Research basis and product implications

Contextual-bandit research supports bounded action selection and offline replay. Delayed-feedback work is relevant because payments and replies arrive later. Safe and offline RL research supports explicit constraints and conservative evaluation when experimentation is costly.

A debt-collection study reports reduced use of harsh actions under deep-RL optimisation, but it studies consumer microloans—not B2B receivables—so its results cannot be transferred directly to this product.

Required event fields include decision ID, context snapshot, eligible actions, selected action and propensity, policy version, human response, execution status, outcome window, reward components and constraint results.

AI Principles · Limitations · Guardrails

Shared principles, applied to collections.

Shared AI experience principles

    Collections application

    • Respect tenant HITL rules, exclusions and email kill switch.
    • Never send outside approved customer contacts or recipient domains.
    • Never surface line items, contacts or actions outside collector authorisation.
    • Draft but do not send when email is disabled.
    • Keep financial tables system-generated from fresh data; do not ask the language model to invent them.
    • Explain risk factors and distinguish overall risk from collector-actionable exposure.
    • Require human judgement for ambiguous disputes, promises and financial consequences.
    • Preserve separate agent-run and account-activity audit trails.
    • Apply tenant-specific writeback rules; NorthStar FSCM contact recording is currently disabled in the documented implementation.
